## The panadapter and waterfall

- **Reading the display:** spectrum above, waterfall below, with dB scales on the spectrum and the waterfall — each draggable to set reference level and range independently (and independently per receiver). The split between spectrum and waterfall is a drag bar; in the G2 layout that split is stored **on the radio**, so every client and every restart shares it.
- **Tuning:** click to tune, drag the spectrum to slide the passband, drag the waterfall to scrub, scroll to step. Tuning feel is smoothed and detent-coalesced so a fast front-panel spin lands where you stop, without overshoot.
- **CTUN (click-tune):** locks the spectrum in place and moves the passband within it — the classic "spectrum stays, you move" mode. Available on the transport bar and as a G2 drawer key.
- **Zoom and pan:** ZOOM controls dock bottom-right (per receiver in the G2 layout); pinch works on touch. A waterfall **SPEED** multiplier sits alongside.
- **Spectrum resolution (FFT):** Settings → DISPLAY (performance sheet) selects the RX analyzer FFT size — 8k, **16k (default)**, 32k, 64k. At Saturn's 1.536 MHz display rate 16k ≈ 94 Hz/bin, 64k ≈ 23 Hz/bin — finer bins resolve individual carriers in a CW pileup or FT8 sub-band; the trade is a slower-filling display (keying smears wider on the waterfall). One instrument: the setting is shared by both receivers and all clients, persists on the radio, applies live.
- **Signal Pop:** weak-signal enhancement that lifts marginal traces out of the noise floor visually (DSP tab → Signal Intelligence: Pop / Snap / Markers). **Snap-to-signal** pulls a click onto the nearest carrier; **peak markers** flag the strongest signals.
- **Notch:** manual notch filters are placed and dragged directly on the display; the auto-notch (ANF) lives with the DSP controls.
- **Band overlay:** a licence-class band overlay shades where you may transmit, and an audible band-edge alert warns as you tune out. The **BAND PLAN** settings tab is the editor, and the transmit **band guard** refuses TX outside your plan.
- **Waterfall speed (SPD):** each G2 pane has an **SPD** pill that cycles that receiver's waterfall speed ×½ → ×1 → ×2 → ×4. The choice is remembered per receiver by the radio itself, so it survives a restart of ANAN Core or the radio.
- **Display effects (Settings → Display → EFFECTS):** **Off**, **Subtle** (default) or **Full**, remembered by the radio.
  - *Subtle* colours the panadapter trace and its fill through the waterfall palette — a signal is the same colour on the trace as in the waterfall, while the noise floor keeps your trace colour on every band (the floor is tracked from the trace itself, so a noisy 40 m floor stays your colour just like a quiet 20 m one) — dims the waterfall outside your receive filter (about 30%, soft edge) so your eye lands on what you are hearing, and rolls the VFO digits like an odometer: a tick per encoder detent, a roll on a retune, a full spin on a band jump.
  - *Full* adds **phosphor persistence** to the panadapter, like a real-time spectrum analyser: every sweep leaves a short glow (it halves in about a quarter of a second), so steady carriers burn in hot, pulsed QRM and hoppers that a plain trace hides stay visible. The noise floor itself leaves no glow — only what rises a few dB above it does — so weak signals are never buried under a burnt-in band; the thin live trace still shows the floor. In Full the live trace is drawn in your single trace colour (the glow carries the palette) with a faint fill under it, and a narrow clear gap is kept around it, so it always reads as the line — crisp on top of its own glow, even inside an SSB signal. The glow restarts whenever you tune, zoom or change the dB range (it is drawn in screen space). The spotlight deepens to 40%.
  - The **filter displays** (the filter card and ribbon mini-panadapter) follow the same setting: Subtle colours their trace and heat fill through the palette with the floor in your trace colour; Full adds the same floor-gated phosphor glow with a clear gap around the live trace, which stays your single trace colour (the peak-hold ghost steps aside, since the glow already remembers recent peaks). Off keeps their classic look.

### Filter display views

Each filter display (the filter card on the G2 and the ribbon's mini-panadapter) can show more than the passband. Pick a view with the **chip** in its top-right corner, which opens a grid of views; **swipe** left or right on an audio view; use the **mouse wheel over the page dots** under it (or **Shift+wheel** on the display); or put the **FLT VIEW** key on a G2 deck with ✎ (press for the next view, hold for Passband). Each receiver remembers its own view on the radio.

- **Passband** — the RF spectrum around the dial with the filter edges (the classic view, and the only one where you drag filter edges).
- **Audio spectrum** — what you actually hear, 0–4 kHz after demodulation, noise reduction and notches, with the filter's audio edges marked.
- **Scope** — a triggered audio oscilloscope with clip lines: clipping, hum, AGC pumping and impulse noise at a glance.
- **Audio waterfall** — an audio spectrogram of the last few seconds in your waterfall palette: tones, heterodynes, CW pitch and speech energy.
- **CW pitch** (CW modes only) — the received tone against your sidetone pitch; centre the bar to zero-beat. Leaving CW shows the audio spectrum and returning restores CW pitch.
- **Levels** — AF level with peak hold, signal strength and AGC gain.

While you transmit, the audio views switch to your **TX audio** (the chip shows **TX**): the mic spectrum, scope and waterfall, and Levels shows MIC, ALC and compression. They return to the receiver when you unkey. The radio computes the views itself and only while one is on screen, so they work identically on the G2 screen, a PC and a remote session. In FreeDV the audio views show the decoded speech.
  - If your device asks for reduced motion, the digits do not roll at any level. Signal Pop, when on, does its own colouring and the palette trace and phosphor stand aside.
- **3D panadapter:** each G2 pane carries a **3D** pill (beside SPD) toggling the WebGPU heightfield renderer. On machines whose browser can't complete WebGPU init (including the Pi's own screen today), the pill shows **3D✕** with an explanatory tooltip and the proven 2D display keeps running — displays never gamble at boot.
- **Wideband display:** the wideband (full-ADC) spectrum panel with its own viewport controls is available on Protocol-2 boards (DISPLAY tab hosts its settings).
- **Backgrounds:** plain, **Beam Map** (world map with QRZ contacts and rotator heading behind the trace), or your own image (Fit/Fill/Stretch) — DISPLAY tab, §21.
